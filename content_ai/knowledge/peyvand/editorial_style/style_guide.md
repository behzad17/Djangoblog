# Peyvand Editorial Style Guide

Official editorial handbook for Peyvand content. Knowledge only—no model instructions.

## Writing standards
- Prioritise clarity for Persian-speaking readers navigating Swedish systems.
- Separate facts, context, and community experience.
- Do not present unofficial claims as law.

## Grammar and register
- Prefer clear contemporary Persian; avoid unnecessary calques from Swedish/English.
- Keep sentences readable on mobile.

## Persian typography
- Use standard Persian punctuation conventions consistently within a piece.
- Avoid mixed RTL/LTR chaos in headlines; keep Swedish names intact.

## Numbers, dates, currency
- Prefer unambiguous date forms (e.g. 25 July 2026 / ۲۰۲۶).
- Use SEK / kronor carefully; spell out when helpful for newcomers.
- Phone numbers and authority names stay accurate.

## Quotations
- Attribute clearly.
- Do not fabricate quotes from agencies.

## Swedish organisation names
- Keep official Swedish names (Skatteverket, Migrationsverket, …).
- Add Persian equivalent from the glossary on first mention when useful.

## Headline rules
- Specific over vague.
- No clickbait that misstates agency actions.
- Include locality only when true.

## Lead paragraph rules
- Answer what happened / what readers need to know first.
- Name the relevant authority early when the topic is institutional.

## SEO conventions
- Natural language first; no keyword stuffing.
- Match category and search intent honestly.

## Forbidden translations
- Do not invent literal translations for agency names when a glossary form exists.
- Avoid fear-driven framing around enforcement topics.

## Preferred wording
- Follow `peyvand/terminology/glossary.md`.
- Prefer “may”, “often”, “depending on the case” over false certainty.

## Editorial consistency
- One term per concept per article after first mention.
- Cross-link related knowledge docs instead of duplicating long explanations.
